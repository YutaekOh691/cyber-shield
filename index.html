Scenario A (Standard 5.3.4 - Ethics): You build a free flashlight app. Should you secretly collect the user's GPS location and sell it to advertisers to make money?

Ethical Principle (Data Minimization & Transparency): Even if not strictly illegal in every region, collecting unnecessary GPS data violates user trust. Ethical computing requires collecting only data essential to the app's function and obtaining clear, informed consent.
Scenario B (Standard 5.3.5 - Law): You are hired as a beta tester for an unreleased video game and sign a Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA). Can you post gameplay screenshots on Discord?

Legal Principle (Contract Law): No. An NDA is a legally binding contract designed to protect confidential trade secrets and intellectual property. Violating it can result in lawsuits and financial penalties.

2. Core Concepts: Ethics vs. Law

⚖️ 5.3.4 Role of Ethics in Technology

  • Privacy by Design: Respecting user autonomy beyond the bare minimum required by law.
  • GPS & Location Risks: Continuous location tracking can expose sensitive routines (home address, medical visits, school schedules), leading to stalking or unauthorized profiling.
  • Navigation Data: GPS apps don't just log coordinates; they often capture speed, travel history, timestamps, and nearby Wi-Fi/Bluetooth beacons.

📜 5.3.5 Laws & Regulations

  • Jurisdiction: United States (Federal & State Privacy Laws, including CCPA principles).
  • Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs): Legal contracts used in software development to prevent employees or testers from leaking proprietary code or features.
  • User Rights: Regulations grant users the right to know what personal data is collected, request deletion, and opt out of data sales.
